# bio_medical_module

Safety boundary: This compressed Vortex Atoms AI extension provides educational biomedical, clinical pharmacology, veterinary science, and avian genetics reference logic. It is not a diagnostic system, prescription system, emergency triage replacement, or substitute for licensed human or veterinary care.

## Human general medicine map
- Systems framework: cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, hepatic, endocrine, neurologic, gastrointestinal, hematologic, infectious disease, dermatologic, musculoskeletal, psychiatric, reproductive, and immunologic reasoning.
- Clinical reasoning: problem representation, differential diagnosis ranking, red-flag recognition, pre-test probability, sensitivity/specificity, likelihood ratios, contraindication checks, follow-up thresholds, and escalation logic.
- Safety principles: do not delay emergency care for chest pain, stroke signs, severe dyspnea, anaphylaxis, sepsis signs, suicidal ideation, severe dehydration, altered mental status, major trauma, or rapidly worsening symptoms.

## Clinical pharmacology map
- Pharmacokinetics: absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination, half-life, steady state, loading concepts, renal/hepatic impairment, protein binding, therapeutic index, and concentration-response uncertainty.
- Pharmacodynamics: agonism, antagonism, partial agonism, inverse agonism, receptor reserve, tolerance, tachyphylaxis, additive toxicity, synergistic toxicity, and pharmacogenomic variability.
- Medication safety: allergies, contraindications, pregnancy/lactation constraints, age extremes, QT risk, serotonin toxicity risk, CNS depression stacking, anticoagulation bleeding risk, nephrotoxicity, hepatotoxicity, and cytochrome P450 interactions.

## Veterinary and avian medicine map
- Species-aware framework: mammals, reptiles, birds, and exotic species differ in metabolism, thermoregulation, stress physiology, analgesic tolerance, fluid needs, and safe handling.
- Avian health logic: birds mask illness; urgent flags include open-mouth breathing, tail bobbing, fluffed posture, anorexia, weight loss, neurologic signs, trauma, egg binding signs, bleeding, collapse, or heat stress.
- Husbandry model: diet, calcium/vitamin D balance, UV exposure, ventilation, enclosure hygiene, enrichment, quarantine, parasite monitoring, and disease transmission controls.

## Avian genetics core probability engine
- Genetic symbols: autosomal dominant A/a, autosomal recessive r/r, incomplete/co-dominant I/i with dosage effect, sex-linked recessive Z^m/Z or Z^m/W in birds where males are ZZ and females are ZW, polygenic modifiers Pn, and epistatic masks E.
- Probability engine: enumerate gametes with allele frequencies, form Punnett products, apply sex chromosome logic, evaluate penetrance, apply epistasis/masking order, then map genotype to phenotype labels.
- Common formulas: autosomal recessive split x split gives 25% visual, 50% split, 25% normal; visual recessive x normal gives 100% split; visual recessive x split gives 50% visual and 50% split; sex-linked recessive visual male x normal female gives all sons split and all daughters visual; normal male split sex-linked x normal female gives 50% split sons and 50% visual daughters.

## Budgie mutation knowledge base
- Hagoromo: Japanese frill phenotype with crest/frill expression; model as autosomal crest/frill complex with variable expressivity and breeder-line modifiers. Track crest genotype, frill expression modifiers, viability, feather quality, and line-breeding risk.
- Blackwing: wing melanin distribution phenotype; model as line-specific modifier interacting with normal/clearwing/greywing-like wing dilution pathways. Track wing-body contrast, melanin retention, and modifier uncertainty.
- Opaline: classic sex-linked recessive budgie mutation. Male is visual when both Z chromosomes carry opaline; female is visual when her single Z carries opaline. Split males can transmit opaline without showing it; females cannot be split for Z-linked recessive traits.
- Rainbow budgie: composite phenotype requiring blue-series base plus opaline plus clearwing or fullbody greywing family plus yellowface/goldenface expression depending on standard. Treat Rainbow as a phenotype assembly rule rather than one allele.
- Budgie probability rule: evaluate base color series first, then sex-linked opaline, then wing dilution family, then yellowface/goldenface modifiers, then violet/dark/grey factors, then pattern modifiers such as spangle/dominant pied/recessive pied if present.

## Finch and small-bird genetics map
- Finch mutations vary by species; inheritance models can differ between zebra finches, society finches, Gouldian finches, canaries, and other passerines. Store species namespace with each allele.
- Zebra finch examples: sex-linked traits require ZZ/ZW logic; autosomal recessives require carrier tracking; orange/brown/black breast and cheek traits may combine with modifiers and selection lines.
- Gouldian finch examples: head color, breast color, and body color can involve sex-linked, autosomal, and modifier interactions; probability engines should keep head/breast/body loci separate before phenotype naming.
- Breeding ethics: avoid high-risk pairings that increase deformity, poor feathering, immune weakness, lethal-factor risk, or severe inbreeding coefficients.
